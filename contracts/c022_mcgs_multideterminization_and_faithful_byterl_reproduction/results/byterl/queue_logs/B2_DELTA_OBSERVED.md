# The b2 delta is visible in the queue metrics, live

`FIDELITY_RULES §4` defines B2 as "B1.5 with bounded blocking FIFO and balanced actor
production/learner consumption". `CONTRACT.md §3.4` says c021 lacked it. This is what its absence
looks like when it is actually instrumented.

## BR0 conformance run, 262 seconds in

```json
{"updates": 352, "consumed_decisions": 4773, "produced_decisions": 189225,
 "queue_occupancy": 6813, "policy_lag": 10.5, "queue_age_s": 129.1, ...}
```

- **production/consumption ratio ≈ 40**. Six actors produced 189,225 environment decisions while
  the learner consumed 4,773.
- **queue occupancy 6,813** and climbing, against BR0's effectively unbounded capacity of 65,536.
- **queue age 129 seconds.** A sample the learner pulls was generated by a policy over two
  minutes old.
- **policy lag 10.5 versions.**

That is the pathology, not a bug. BR0/BR1/BR1_5 run the same actor–learner machinery with an
unbounded queue and no production throttle, exactly so that the b2 rung has something to fix.
Actors race ahead, the queue fills with stale trajectories, and by the time the learner reaches
a sample its behaviour policy is ten versions and two minutes behind — which is precisely the
off-policy regime V-trace's importance ratio has to correct for, at a magnitude that makes the
correction do real work. `rho_clipped_upper_frac` is 0.516 here: more than half the samples are
clipped.

## What BR2 and BR3 must show

Bounded (48) and blocking. Producers block when full, so:

- occupancy oscillates below capacity rather than climbing;
- production/consumption settles near 1;
- queue age falls to the order of a second;
- policy lag falls with it.

The BR3 smoke run already showed this shape: occupancy 2–8 against a capacity of 8, ratio 1.0097,
queue age 0.89 s.

## Why this is evidence and not just a log line

`PROBE_MATRIX B09` asks for "producer/consumer ratio and queue age remain controlled", and it is
the kind of requirement that is easy to satisfy vacuously — a system with no queue at all reports
no imbalance. c021 gathered whole iterations synchronously, so it had nothing to report and
nothing to fix, which is why the b2 rung had no meaning there.

Here the ratio moves from ~40 to ~1 across the rung boundary, in the same codebase, changing one
flag. That is the delta the published ladder names, measured rather than asserted.

**A caution about reading it as an improvement.** A ratio near 1 means the queue is doing its
job; it does not by itself mean BR2 plays better than BR1.5. `TRAINING_AND_EVALUATION §2`
requires the rung comparison at an EQUAL sample budget before any such claim, and that is what
the controlled runs are for. The queue metric is evidence that the rung's *mechanism* is present
and working, not evidence about its effect.

## One practical consequence

At a 40× overproduction ratio, BR0/BR1/BR1_5 burn actor CPU generating trajectories that are
discarded unconsumed. That is inherent to the rung and is not "fixed" — fixing it would BE the
b2 change and would erase the rung distinction. Actor count is held constant across all five
rungs for the same reason: `MANDATORY_IMPLEMENTATION B3` permits tuning actor count "only to
keep production/consumption close to one **without changing semantics**", and that tuning belongs
to B2 and above, not to the rungs whose defining property is that they do not do it.
