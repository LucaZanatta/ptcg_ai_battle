    contrast table is the scoreboard -- prize lead IS the score, and a small bench is downstream of
    being knocked out. Listed in full anyway, because suppressing the tautological rows would make
    the others look better than they are.

    were the games where we attacked LATE (2.80 vs 2.24). On the real ladder the sign flips: we
    attack earlier in losses (2.70 vs 2.98) and more often (6.23 vs 5.79). F5 is a property of that
    panel matchup, not of how the champion loses on the ladder.

    Mega Lucario losses end on turn 7.5 against 12.3 in wins, Grimmsnarl 9.0 against 14.0. The
    mechanism is arithmetic in the card text -- Phantom Dive does 200 into 320 and 340 HP attackers
    that hit back for 270 and 180+30, and a knocked-out Mega gives them three prizes to our two.
    
    It is a deck fact, and the deck is fixed by the archetype. No override, no score constant and
    no turn-level search changes what 200 damage does to 340 hit points. That is one mechanism for
    the whole campaign's negative result rather than five separate shrugs.
    
    Quiet-window latency, single process: mean 0.29 ms, median per-game p99 0.31 ms. The 1,378 ms
    outlier in the contended final panel was contention, as R8 said it would be.

    c023: D7 -- the acceptance rule is asymmetric, and both acceptances prove it
    
    Twenty-three rounds, two acceptances, and both fired on the two LOWEST incumbent draws in the
    run. r22's winning child scored 0.5240, which is below the mean best child of 0.5245: it did
    not win by being good, it won because the incumbent drew its minimum of 0.4750.
    
    The rule is best_child - incumbent >= margin with the incumbent measured once per round. That
    controls for machine conditions but not for the incumbent's own sampling noise -- the child
    side already takes a maximum over five draws while the incumbent side is a single draw, so a
    low incumbent is worth exactly as much as a high child. It should have compared against the
    incumbent's running mean, whose standard error after twenty rounds is ~0.2 points rather than
    ~1.0.
    
    The protocol is not changed mid-run; choosing a rule after seeing which rounds it accepts is
    the thing the registration exists to prevent. The search finishes under the registered rule,
    the post-hoc analysis uses the pooled incumbent series, and the confirm and validate stages on
    fresh runs are out of the defect's reach.
    
    Three of the four accepted parameters point the same way as F5: reach harder for a way to
    attack when the main line has not arrived (Latias ex fetch, Crispin fetch, a smaller penalty
    for promoting the backup body).
